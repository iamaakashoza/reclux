How it works

Four steps. No learning curve.

Click Reclux in your toolbar. Pick Normal or Webcam, the quality and the sound, then press Start recording.

Your browser asks what to share: a tab, a window or your whole screen. After a 5-second countdown it records. Pause, resume or stop from the small controls window.

Stop takes you straight to the editor. The cursor zoom is already there. Cut what you do not need and press Save changes.

Name your video and press Export video. Choose any folder or drive. It is an ordinary MP4 that plays everywhere.

Download

Add Reclux to your browser.

Reclux is not in the browser stores, so you add it by hand. It takes about a minute, and you only need the .zip for your browser.

Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera

One file for all four. Same steps. Stays installed through restarts.

Download .zip · 452 KB

reclux-extension-v2.zip · Version 2.0.0 · Chrome 116 or newer

  1. Extract the .zip into a folder you will keep. Reclux runs from that folder, so deleting it removes Reclux.
  2. Open the extensions page. Paste this into the address bar: chrome://extensions
  3. Turn on Developer mode. The switch is at the top right.
  4. Click Load unpacked and choose the folder you extracted.
  5. Done. The welcome guide opens by itself. Pin Reclux to your toolbar to keep it close.

Firefox

Its own file. Fewer steps. You add it again each time Firefox starts.

Download .zip · 452 KB

reclux-firefox-extension-v2.zip · Version 2.0.0 · Firefox 140 or newer

  1. Keep the .zip as it is. Firefox reads it directly, so there is nothing to extract.
  2. Open the debugging page. Paste this into the address bar: about:debugging#/runtime/this-firefox
  3. Click Load Temporary Add-on… and choose the .zip.
  4. Done. The welcome guide opens by itself.

Why Firefox has its own file

Firefox handles extensions and video recording differently from the other browsers, so Reclux needs a separate build. Why it goes away. Firefox keeps only add-ons signed by Mozilla. Reclux is not signed, so Firefox removes it when it closes. What is built differently. Firefox has no AAC sound encoder and no built-in frame reader, so this build brings its own frame reader and saves MP4 with Opus sound. What you will notice. Microphone only, no system audio. One extra click to choose what to share. It cannot keep your PC awake. 720p is the smoother choice.

FAQ

Asked and answered.

Is Reclux free?

Yes. There is no account, no trial and nothing to pay.

Where is my recording kept?

On your own computer. While you record and edit, it is kept in the browser’s private storage on your drive. When you press Export video, you choose the folder or drive. It is never uploaded.

Does it need the internet?

No. Reclux works with the internet off. You only need a connection to download the .zip.

Can I edit my videos?

Yes. Split, trim and delete parts, remove zooms, and undo any step. Saving builds a new video and replaces the original only once the new one is complete.

Do the webcam circle and cursor zoom see my web pages?

Reclux asks for access to web pages only when you pick Webcam, never at install. The circle is shown on top of the page, and cursor zoom notes where your pointer is while you record. Nothing is collected or sent anywhere.

Does it keep recording when I switch apps or tabs?

Yes. Record, then work anywhere. Pin the small controls window to keep it on top, so you can pause or stop.

What happens if my browser or PC crashes?

Reclux writes the video to disk in small pieces as you record. Next time you open it, it offers to recover the recording.

Why do I need Developer mode?

Browsers add extensions automatically only from their own stores. Reclux is not in a store, so you add it once with Load unpacked, which needs Developer mode. Firefox uses its debugging page instead.

How do I update from version 1?

Extract the new .zip over the same folder, then press reload on Reclux in the extensions page. In Firefox, add the new .zip next time. Your settings carry over.

Does it work on tablets or a phone?

Reclux runs on desktop and laptop computers. Phones and tablets are not supported for now.

Is the code public?

Yes. You can read all of it on GitHub.
